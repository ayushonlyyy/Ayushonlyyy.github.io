THE IDH

Information Dimension Hypothesis

The Information Dimension Hypothesis (IDH) proposes that the Internet — the global network of interconnected information — constitutes a genuine, emergent dimension of physical reality, not merely a technological artifact but a fundamental stratum of existence.

Drawing from John Archibald Wheeler's principle that physical reality is fundamentally informational ("It from Bit"), IDH extends this insight to the networked information layer humanity has constructed. Just as spacetime emerged as the fabric of physical reality in Einstein's relativity, an infosphere may have crystallized as a new dimensional substrate.

The Holographic Principle — which suggests that the information content of a volume of space is encoded on its boundary — provides further scaffolding. If reality is holographic at its deepest level, the Internet's boundary-encoded information structures may mirror this fundamental architecture.

Shannon's Information Theory offers the mathematical backbone: defining information as reduction of uncertainty, IDH treats bits flowing across the Internet as physically meaningful — causally efficacious events that shape the real world as concretely as electromagnetic forces or gravitational fields.

The hypothesis remains open, actively developed, and invites cross-disciplinary scrutiny from physicists, information theorists, and philosophers of mind.
